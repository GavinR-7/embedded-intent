## Positioning decisions

**One site, one brand.** Web development and AI automation are one ladder sold
to one buyer, not two businesses.

**AI leads, outcome attached.** Every AI claim names the thing it does for the
owner. "AI that answers your phone at 9pm," never "AI-powered solutions."
The word AI opens the door; the outcome is what gets signed.

**Website is the entry, automation is the margin.** Web builds are being
commoditized. Automation retainers are stickier, higher-perceived-value, and
recur. Structure the offer so the site is how they start and the automation is
where the relationship goes.

**Geography: neutral brand, local content.** Nothing in the name or domain
locks to Long Island. Long Island appears in copy as home base, plus per-area
service pages added as clients land there (cantandoDigital does exactly this:
/web-design-chester-county-pa, /seo-chester-county). Location pages are
additive for local SEO — they never exclude anyone.

## Copy playbook — the ten patterns worth stealing

**1. Negative-space headline.** Define yourself against what's normal and bad.
> "We build websites that don't stop at launch."
> "Most websites are finished on the day they stop being useful."

**2. The disarming admission.** Concede the skepticism before pitching.
> "Everyone agrees AI is useful. Almost nobody has shipped anything."
> "You don't need another AI strategy presentation."

**3. Symptoms in the owner's words, written as scenes.** Not feature bullets.
> "Someone fills out your form at 7pm and hears back at 10am the next day.
> They already booked someone else."
> "You've done hundreds of jobs and have 40 Google reviews. Your competitor
> has 400."

**4. Before/After pairs.** Their AI page runs six. Each is a scene, not a claim.
> **Before:** A lead comes in at 8:40pm. Someone sees it at 9:15 the next
> morning and calls. They've already booked your competitor.
> **After:** An AI assistant replies in under a minute, asks the qualifying
> questions, offers real appointment slots, and puts a briefed lead on the
> right person's calendar.

**5. The comparison table.** Six rows, "Typical agency" vs. them. Concrete
rows only — who does the work, who owns the accounts, what reporting means,
contract length.

**6. Ownership language, repeated.** Attacks the agency-lock-in fear directly.
> "Your accounts, your data, documented. Leave and it still works."
> "Nothing is hostage to the relationship."

**7. The anti-sell.** Naming what they'll talk you *out* of.
> "We'll tell you not to" (automate things that shouldn't be).
> "If a channel or an automation isn't earning its keep, we say so."
This buys more credibility than any claim on the page.

**8. Outcome metrics, not activity metrics.** Stated as what they measure:
qualified leads, speed to first response, hours returned, cost per acquired
customer. Explicitly rejects impressions, clicks, reach.

**9. Low-friction CTA with a guaranteed takeaway.**
> "Get a free audit"
> "Free · 30 minutes · You leave with a prioritised list either way"

**10. Named specifics everywhere.** Real towns, real client names, real
numbers. "J.A. Cuzzupe Inc. went from 2 leads a month to 10." Vague equals
fabricated in a reader's mind — specificity is itself a trust signal, which is
exactly why invented specifics are so damaging when caught.

## Homepage copy draft

### Hero

**Eyebrow:** AI AUTOMATION & WEBSITES FOR SERVICE BUSINESSES

**H1:** AI that answers your phone at 9pm.

**Sub:** Most of your leads arrive when nobody's there to catch them. We build
the website that brings them in — and the AI that answers, qualifies, and books
them in under a minute. Every time, including Sunday.

**CTA primary:** Get a free 30-minute audit
**CTA secondary:** See how it works

**Trust line:** No contracts · You own every account we build · You work
directly with the person building it

**Hero visual:** the live lead-journey chain, animated — `New lead captured →
AI qualifies & replies → Routed & booked → Review request sent`, with a
"Manual steps: 0" counter. Not a laptop mockup.

### Section 2 — The problem

**Kicker:** THE REAL PROBLEM

**H2:** You're not losing jobs because you're bad at the work.

**Body:** You already do good work and you already have customers. What's
missing is everything between "someone was interested" and "we got paid and
they told three people." That's a systems problem, and it's fixable.

**Symptom list** (write each as a scene, not a bullet):
- A form comes in Friday at 7pm. You see it Monday. They booked someone else Saturday.
- You've done four hundred jobs and have thirty-one Google reviews.
- Every quote goes out and then waits on whoever remembers to chase it.
- Your phone rings while you're on a roof. That's the whole lead process.
- You know AI could help. Nobody has shown you where it plugs in.

### Section 3 — What we build

**Kicker:** WHAT WE BUILD

**H2:** Start with the website. Add the automation when it's earning.

**Primary card — Website Design & Build.** Custom sites for service
businesses, built to convert — fast on mobile, phone number where a thumb can
reach it, and the system behind it.

**Add-on cards:**
- **AI Lead Response** — answers, qualifies, and books in under a minute, 24/7.
- **Missed-Call Text-Back** — every unanswered call gets a text in ten seconds.
- **Review Automation** — finished job triggers the ask, automatically.
- **Internal AI Assistant** — your pricing, process and paperwork, answerable instantly.

### Section 4 — Before / After

**Kicker:** WHAT CHANGES

Four before/after pairs, pattern #4 above. Use the 8:40pm lead, the review
ask nobody remembers, the quote that never got chased, the twenty questions
every new hire asks.

### Section 5 — How it works

Four steps: **Find** (where the money is leaking) → **Build** (in accounts you
own) → **Automate** (it runs whether or not anyone remembers) → **Measure**
(outcomes, not activity).

### Section 6 — Work

Three real case studies. Photos, what was built, honest numbers only.
See "results integrity" below.

### Section 7 — Pricing

Published on the page. Three tiers plus the audit entry point.

### Section 8 — Why me

The comparison table, adapted. The honest differentiators for a solo
operator: you talk to the person building it, no account manager, no
outsourcing, your accounts and your data, no long-term contract.

### Section 9 — FAQ

Objections to answer, taken from theirs: do I have to buy everything at once ·
I already have a website · how long · who owns the accounts · does it work
with my CRM · what does it actually cost · will AI replace my people (reframe:
volume without hiring) · what happens when the AI gets it wrong (guardrails,
human approval, audit log).

### Section 10 — Close

**H2:** Let's find the fastest win in your business.

**Body:** Thirty minutes. We look at how leads reach you, what happens to the
ones that arrive after hours, and where your team is losing time — then tell
you what to build first and what it costs.

**Micro:** Free · 30 minutes · You leave with a prioritised list either way

## Results integrity (non-negotiable)

Everything on the work page has to be true and checkable. Client permission to
"use anything" is permission from the client — it is not consent from the
prospect being shown the number, and it is not a defense to a deceptive
advertising claim.

What can go on the page today:
- Before/after screenshots (real)
- Load-time and PageSpeed numbers **measured**, old vs. new, once the new
  scores are actually good
- What was built — John's live MLS/IDX pipeline is genuinely differentiating
- Testimonials in the client's own words
- "Launched September 2026 — results tracking in progress"

What cannot: invented lead counts, invented percentages, invented conversion
rates, borrowed screenshots.

Once real tracking is running on Savoretti and Above All, real numbers arrive
on their own within a quarter. Ship honest, backfill real.

## Open blockers

1. **Brand name + domain** — blocks everything downstream.
2. **Above All Tents PageSpeed is 64 mobile / 84 desktop.** Cannot sell speed
   while the flagship portfolio piece scores 64. Fix before launch — almost
   always images (`next/image`, AVIF/WebP, correct sizing) or a heavy hero.
3. **Savoretti launch** — targeted this week.
